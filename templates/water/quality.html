Safe Water

Water Quality

Understand drinking water safety, basic indicators, and when to seek professional testing.

Important: This page provides general awareness only. OPES EDGE cannot certify drinking water safety. For health concerns, always consult a certified laboratory or local health department.
Indicators

Key Quality Parameters

What to look for in safe drinking water.

Parameter Safe Range (BIS) Why It Matters Simple Check
pH Level 6.5 – 8.5 Too acidic or alkaline water can corrode pipes and affect health. Use pH test strips (₹100-200)
TDS Up to 500 mg/L Total Dissolved Solids. High TDS affects taste and may indicate contamination. TDS meter (₹200-500)
Turbidity Up to 1 NTU Cloudiness from suspended particles. High turbidity hides bacteria. Visual clarity check
Chloride Up to 250 mg/L High chloride makes water salty and can corrode metals. Taste test + TDS meter
Fluoride 0.5 – 1.0 mg/L Too much causes dental fluorosis. Too little weakens teeth. Lab test recommended
Nitrate Up to 45 mg/L High nitrate from fertilizers/sewage is dangerous for infants. Lab test recommended
Coliform Bacteria 0 per 100 mL Indicates fecal contamination. Must be zero in drinking water. Lab test required
Iron Up to 0.3 mg/L High iron causes reddish stains, metallic taste, and pipe clogging. Visual + taste check

Source: Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) IS 10500:2012. Values may vary by region and usage type.

DIY

Simple Home Checks

Quick tests anyone can do without special equipment.

Look

Fill a clear glass. Water should be colourless. Brown, yellow, or cloudy water needs attention.

Smell

Water should be odourless. A rotten egg smell indicates hydrogen sulphide. Chlorine smell is normal for treated water.

Taste

Should taste neutral. Salty, metallic, or bitter tastes indicate high dissolved minerals or contamination.

Settle

Let water sit for 30 minutes. Sediment at the bottom suggests suspended particles or pipe corrosion.

Treatment

Purification Methods

Choose the right method for your water source.

Boiling

The simplest method. Boil water for 1 minute (3 minutes at high altitude) to kill bacteria, viruses, and parasites.

Best for: Emergency, rural areas, microbial contamination
RO + UV + UF

Reverse Osmosis removes dissolved salts. UV kills microbes. UF filters particles. The gold standard for urban homes.

Best for: High TDS borewell water, urban homes
Chlorination

Adding chlorine tablets or bleaching powder kills most pathogens. Used by municipalities and in emergency relief.

Best for: Community supplies, disaster relief
Professional Help

When to Get Lab Testing

Some situations require certified laboratory analysis.

After Flooding or Contamination Event

Flood water can carry sewage, chemicals, and industrial waste. Do not drink without testing.

Newborn or Pregnant Woman in Household

Infants are vulnerable to nitrates and bacteria. Test well water before use for formula preparation.

New Borewell or Water Source

Always test a new water source before regular use. Arsenic, fluoride, and heavy metals vary by geology.

Unexplained Health Issues

Stomach illness, skin rashes, or dental staining may indicate water quality problems. Test immediately.

Contact: Reach out to your district water testing laboratory, State Pollution Control Board, or certified private labs like SGS, Eurofins, or local NABL-accredited centres.
